From a URL to evidence in three steps

The agents do the tedious part: shopping the same item many times, as different people.

1

Pick a site and an intent

Choose a site you are allowed to test. The audit follows the booking and cancellation flows a real customer would.

2

Agents shop as different people

Browsers from several countries and devices, as new and returning visitors, record every price and screen. They stop before any payment form.

3

Read the evidence, send the complaint

A transparency score, a price waterfall, screenshots for every finding and a complaint draft that cites only what was measured.

Seven things it measures

Each check is a measurement with evidence, not an opinion.

Drip pricing

The advertised price against what you pay, with every fee itemised.

Basket sneaking

Paid extras that are ticked for you and added to the total.

Fake deadlines

Countdown timers that jump back when the page reloads.

Personalised pricing

Different prices by country, device or visit history, beyond random noise.

Manipulative wording

Scarcity, social proof, guilt-trip and nagging copy, found by a classifier.

Cancellation friction

Clicks to cancel against clicks to sign up.

Policy against reality

Each written promise in the terms, checked against what the audit saw.
